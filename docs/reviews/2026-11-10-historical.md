## Iranian Persian (west2369)
1. Major. Hear it then: the Classical values are reconstructions stated as fact. Replace with: "Scholars reconstruct Classical šēr 'lion' and šīr 'milk' with different vowels. The Arabic script spells both شیر. Three kinds of evidence point to the split. Dari still has [ʃeːɾ] against [ʃiːɾ], and Tajik шер against шир [@wikt-shir]. Hindi borrowed the 'lion' word as शेर /ʃeːɾ/ [@wikt-hi-sher]. Rastorgueva (1982), as summarized in Persian phonology, describes Dari keeping the old eight-vowel system [@wp-persian-phonology]. Iranian Persian merged them. When the merger happened: we do not know from the sources checked. Confidence: high for the contrast, unknown for the date." For و: "Classical و is reconstructed as [w]. Iranian Persian and Tajik moved to [v], not in every position. Dari keeps [w] (Baker 2016)." Label the Classical column "Reconstruction".
2. Major. Stage dates overlap: "Early New Persian (8th to 9th century)" and "Classical Persian (10th to 18th century)", both attributed to "one conventional scheme" [@wp-persian-language]; move Rudaki under Classical. Reconcile with the linguist review's Paul 2013 dating (8th to 12th): present both as different schemes, attributed. Earliest New Persian documents: Judeo-Persian letters in Hebrew script from Dandān-Uiliq, probably 8th century (TODO(verify) in Paul; lead https://geniza.princeton.edu/documents/35176).
3. Major. Middle Persian dated by the empire: "Middle Persian (about the 3rd century BCE to the 9th century CE; Zoroastrian books into the 10th and 11th)", then "It was the official, religious and literary language of the Sasanian empire, 224 to 651 CE." Source https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Persian.
4. Major. Origin of New Persian: "Most accounts follow Gilbert Lazard. New Persian continues the spoken Persian of the Sasanian court, called darī. That speech spread to Khorasan, where it was first written in Arabic script and absorbed Parthian and other northeastern words [@lazard1975]. A debated point is whether New Persian is a later stage of written Middle Persian or a sister dialect [@lazard2003]." Drop "Paul (2000)" until found.
5. Minor. Old Persian signs: "It had 36 sound signs (three vowels and 33 consonant-plus-vowel signs) and a few word signs: five words, written with eight signs if variants count." Sources LRC; de Vaan and Lubotsky 2011.
6. Minor. Behistun as the oldest: add "Inscriptions in the names of earlier kings exist, but most scholars date them later. Many think the script was created for Darius." Stage end "to about 330 BCE" (Kent 1953, p. 6).
7. Minor. Keep "about 520 BCE"; add "The exact date of the Old Persian version is debated; it may have been added after the Elamite and Akkadian texts. TODO(verify) Schmitt 1991; Borger 1982."
8. Minor. Kartir: "Three of the five words are Aramaic spellings: W for ud 'and', ANE for az 'I', ZY for ī. The Persian reading is a scholarly reconstruction." Translation "And I, Kirdir, the mowbed (chief of the magi)"; name the variants Kartir and Kirdīr once.
9. Minor. Ezafe: from Old Persian haya/hya via Middle Persian ī (Iranica "EŻĀFA", https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ezafa; seen via search only, 403; majority view).
10. Minor. India: "TODO(verify): under British rule Persian lost its official role in the 1830s and 1840s; sources differ on the year." Lead Act XXIX of 1837. Do not print 1843 as fact. (The linguist review offers a secondary source for 1837; use it with TODO.)
