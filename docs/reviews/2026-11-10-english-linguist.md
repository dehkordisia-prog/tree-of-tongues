## Required fixes
1. Blocker. Hook "The everyday pronoun they is a borrowing from Old Norse, ...": contested claim stated as settled. Cole (2018, Diachronica 35(2): 165 to 209) argues for a native origin in Old English þā, þāra, þām; her abstract calls the Norse origin the "commonly held" view; her book (The Early History of they, Palgrave 2026) argues Norse contact mainly reinforced a native development. Fix: "The pronoun they is usually traced to Old Norse, though one recent study argues it grew from a native Old English word." Or choose an uncontested hook, e.g. the single medieval copy of Beowulf (fix 7). Sources: https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/a-native-origin-for-present-day-english-they-their-them/ ; https://link.springer.com/book/9783032056849
2. Blocker. Words entry on they: "Most accounts derive Middle English þei from Old Norse þeir, replacing Old English hīe [@cole2018_they]. Add @article{cole2018_they, author={Cole, Marcelle}, year={2018}, title={A native origin for Present-Day English they, their, them}, journal={Diachronica}, volume={35}, number={2}, pages={165--209}, doi={10.1075/dia.16026.col}}.
